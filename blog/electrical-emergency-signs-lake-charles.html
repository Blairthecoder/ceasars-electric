Is This an Electrical Emergency? Signs You Should Call Right Away

JC
Jevante Ceasar
Owner, Ceasar’s Electric. September 20, 2026

Not every electrical hiccup means calling an electrician at midnight. But some problems genuinely cannot wait, and waiting on the wrong one can mean a house fire, a serious shock, or thousands of dollars in damage that a same-day call would have prevented. Here is how to tell the difference, especially useful during Lake Charles storm season when outages and power surges are common.

Call Immediately: These Are True Emergencies

  • Burning smell from an outlet, switch, or the panel. Stop using the area and turn off the circuit if you can do so safely, then call right away. This is one of the clearest fire warning signs there is.
  • Sparking outlets or switches. Sparks mean arcing inside the wiring or device, which generates heat well past the point of safety.
  • A warm, hot, or buzzing panel. The panel should never feel warm to the touch or make audible noise. This often points to loose connections or overheating.
  • Exposed or damaged wiring. Chewed wires, wiring exposed after storm damage, or wires you can see through cracked drywall are a shock hazard.
  • You felt a shock from an appliance, switch, or outlet. Even a mild shock means something is wired incorrectly or grounded improperly, and it will not fix itself.
  • Total loss of power that is not a wider outage. If your neighbors have power and you do not, the problem is likely in your own service or panel.
  • Water intrusion near your panel or outlets. Flooding is a serious risk during Southwest Louisiana storms. See the note on flooding below before touching anything.

What to Do While You Wait for Help

If it is safe to do so, shut off the breaker that controls the affected circuit rather than the main breaker for the whole house. Never touch a panel, outlet, or switch with wet hands or while standing in water. If there is any flooding near your panel, do not enter the area at all until an electrician or the utility company confirms it is safe.

Usually Fine to Schedule: These Can Wait

  • A single breaker that trips once and resets normally. This is often just a circuit doing its job during a temporary overload.
  • An outlet that stopped working but shows no other symptoms. This is worth a look through our troubleshooting and repair service, not necessarily an emergency visit.
  • Lights that flicker occasionally, without dimming across the whole house. Still worth mentioning at your next scheduled visit.
  • An outdated panel or old wiring with no active symptoms. If you are seeing early warning signs, read up on panel upgrade signs and plan the work rather than treating it as urgent.
  • GFCI or AFCI outlets that trip occasionally. Learn what is normal in our guide to GFCI vs AFCI protection.

Why It Matters to Get This Right

Ignoring a true emergency risks a fire or serious injury. Treating every minor issue as an emergency runs up costs unnecessarily. The judgment call is easier when you know the specific warning signs, and when in doubt, a quick call costs nothing and gives you a clear answer from someone who does this work every day.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I smell burning from an outlet?+
Stop using that outlet immediately, turn off the breaker that controls it if you can safely identify it, and call an electrician right away. A burning smell from an outlet or panel is one of the clearest signs of an electrical fire hazard and should never be ignored.
Is a tripped breaker an emergency?+
A single tripped breaker that resets normally is usually not an emergency. A breaker that trips repeatedly, will not reset, or trips along with a burning smell or buzzing sound is a sign of a bigger problem and worth a same-day call.
When should I call an emergency electrician instead of waiting?+
Call immediately for a burning smell, sparking outlets or switches, a warm or buzzing panel, exposed wiring, a shock from touching an appliance or switch, or a total loss of power that is not a utility-wide outage.
Should I turn off my main breaker during a storm?+
Not usually. Your panel and breakers are designed to handle normal storm conditions. The exception is flooding: if water is rising toward your panel or outlets, shut off power at the main breaker only if you can reach it safely without standing in water, and call an electrician afterward before restoring power.
