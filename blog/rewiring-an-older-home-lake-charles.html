Rewiring

Rewiring an older home in Lake Charles: what to know

JC
Jevante Ceasar
Owner, Ceasar’s Electric. September 18, 2026

Lake Charles has a lot of housing stock built before modern electrical code existed, and a lot of it still carries the original wiring. If your home has cloth-covered wiring, aluminum branch circuits, or knob-and-tube in the attic, rewiring is not a cosmetic upgrade. It is a safety project that removes real fire risk from your walls.

Here is how to know if your home needs it, what the process actually looks like, and what to expect from start to finish.

Signs your home needs to be rewired

  • Two-prong outlets throughout the home, meaning no ground wire is present
  • Breakers or fuses that trip repeatedly on ordinary loads
  • Outlets or switch plates that feel warm or show scorch marks
  • Lights that flicker or dim independent of the AC or major appliances
  • Cloth-covered, aluminum, or knob-and-tube wiring visible in the attic or basement
  • A burning or hot plastic smell with no obvious source

If you are seeing several of these together, it is worth having a licensed electrician take a look before it becomes an emergency. We cover the panel-specific version of these warning signs in our post on signs your home needs a panel upgrade.

What the rewiring process actually involves

Most homeowners picture a rewire as gutting every wall in the house, but that is rarely how it works. We use fishing techniques to run new copper wiring through existing walls and cavities, which keeps drywall damage to a minimum. We work room by room so the home stays livable, and we coordinate the utility disconnect only for the final tie-in.

A typical rewire includes removing outdated or unsafe wiring, running new grounded copper circuits, updating outlets and switches, adding required GFCI and AFCI protection, and labeling every circuit at the panel. If you are unclear on the difference between those two protections, our guide on GFCI vs AFCI explains what each one does and where code requires it.

How long does it take?

Timeline depends on the size and layout of the home, but most whole-home rewires take several days to about a week. Partial rewires, like correcting aluminum wiring in a kitchen or addressing one problem area, are often finished in a single day. We give you a clear timeline with your written quote before work starts.

Rewiring and your panel often go together

If your home needs a rewire, there is a good chance the panel is also outdated or undersized for how the home is used today. Since the work overlaps, most homeowners choose to handle a panel upgrade at the same time as a rewire rather than opening the walls twice. It is also a smart time to plan ahead for future loads like an EV charger or a standby generator, since the capacity is already being reworked.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my Lake Charles home needs to be rewired?+
Warning signs include cloth-covered, aluminum, or knob-and-tube wiring, breakers that trip often, discolored or warm outlets, flickering lights, and a persistent burning smell. Homes built before the 1970s are the most common candidates.
Do I have to move out of my house during a rewire?+
Usually not. We work room by room and use fishing techniques to run new wire with minimal drywall damage, so most homeowners stay in the house during a whole-home rewire.
Does rewiring my house also mean I need a panel upgrade?+
Often, yes. A rewire is the natural time to size your panel correctly for modern loads, since the walls are already open and adding capacity later is more disruptive.
Is aluminum wiring actually dangerous?+
Aluminum branch wiring can loosen and overheat at connections over time, which is a recognized fire risk. We can replace it entirely or correct the terminations to make the circuits safe.
